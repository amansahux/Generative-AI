The peacock is one of the most visually striking birds in the world, celebrated for its brilliant iridescent plumage and elaborate courtship display. Native to South Asia, particularly India and Sri Lanka, the male peacock, known for its long train of feathers covered in eye-like ocelli, fans this train into a dazzling semicircle to attract peahens during mating season. Its body shimmers in shades of blue, green, and gold due to microscopic feather structures that refract light rather than pigments alone. Peafowl are omnivorous, feeding on insects, small reptiles, seeds, and plants. Despite their heavy tails, peacocks can fly short distances and often roost in trees at night for safety. In India, the peacock holds cultural and religious significance, symbolizing beauty, grace, pride, and divinity, and it was officially declared the national bird of India in 1963. Peahens, by contrast, are much duller in coloration, typically brownish or grey, which helps them stay camouflaged while nesting and protecting their eggs from predators. Peacocks are known for their loud, distinctive calls, especially before monsoon rains, which some cultures interpret as a sign of impending weather change. They are social birds, often found in small groups called musters, and can live up to twenty years in the wild or longer in captivity under proper care.
